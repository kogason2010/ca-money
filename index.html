About these numbers
  • The source is Open FI$Cal, the state's financial transparency site. It publishes every expenditure transaction from the FI$Cal accounting system, one file per month.
  • Open FI$Cal covers about 79% of the state's budgetary expenditures (151 departments, 188 business units). Some large departments are not in it at all, including the Department of Transportation (Caltrans), the Department of Motor Vehicles and the Department of Corrections and Rehabilitation, so their agencies look much smaller here than in the state budget.
  • Departments moved onto FI$Cal over several years, so the earliest years include far fewer departments. The site flags those years.
  • Data is published with a lag of at least 60 days, and adjustments can still be posted to a month after it ends, so the newest months may change.
  • Every monthly file Open FI$Cal has posted is read, including recent ones that are not yet on its download list. Each transaction is counted in the fiscal year recorded on it, and amounts are added up to the cent.
  • Amounts are net: refunds, reversals and negative entries such as internal cost recovery reduce the totals, and labels show these net amounts. A chart can't draw a negative band, so a fund's net-negative amount for a department is left out of the drawing (the total left out is listed under the chart), and in agency and department views negative spending types are drawn as a "Negative entries" band flowing in.
  • Spending types are the state's account categories. Hover over one for its definition, based on the State Administrative Manual (section 7670).
  • Names of funds, agencies and departments are shown as Open FI$Cal abbreviates them, with a few abbreviations spelled out.

About these numbers
  • The source is the State Controller's Office By the Numbers site, which publishes each city's and county's annual Financial Transactions Report. Figures are posted as each local government submitted them; the State Controller's Office does not vouch for their accuracy.
  • Totals here are day-to-day spending on services from governmental funds ("current expenditures"). They leave out city-run businesses such as water, electric, airport and hospital enterprise funds. Capital projects and debt payments are shown separately.
  • Who provides a service varies. A city with little or no fire, library or sewer spending may be served by a county department or a special district, which report separately. Many cities also contract with their county sheriff for police.
  • Per-resident amounts divide spending by the population estimate in the same report. In cities with very few residents (and large businesses) per-resident figures can be extreme.
  • Each service area adds up specific report lines; "What's in each total" lists them for the selected place.
  • Only years that use the State Controller's current report layout are shown; earlier reports group spending differently and are left out rather than compared unevenly.

Data: Open FI$Cal (California Department of FI$Cal) and By the Numbers (California State Controller's Office). Updated monthly by an automated job.
